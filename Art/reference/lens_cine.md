# 蓝白 / Lanbai: LENS & COLOUR (cinematography + grade)

Role: cinematographer + colourist. Scope: every scene 1 站牌 to 12 醒 and every transition. It covers what each frame is,
where the eye goes from the bus seat, how each scene is lit, its palette, its atmosphere, its PPv2 grade, and the value
score that makes the grade tell the story. The world's spine is 蓝/白.

Everything numeric here was checked through a numpy emulation of the PPv2 3.x colour path (HDR grading and the Neutral
tonemapper: `art/lens_cine/ppv2.py`). A value in this file is therefore a prediction of what the screen will show, not
a guess. Companion files:

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `art/lens_cine/grades.json` | every PPv2 profile, machine-readable (for an editor script that writes the `.asset` profiles) |
| `art/lens_cine/calib_out.md`, `calib.json` | the full calibration tables (section 11), generated by `calib.py` |
| `art/lens_cine/value_score.png`, `value_score.json` | the brightness / colour-temperature / dynamics score and the film barcode (section 4) |
| `art/lens_cine/mock/*_graded.png` | grade mock-ups built from the delivered cloud cards and tentacle renders (section 10) |
| `ppv2.py`, `calib.py`, `mock.py`, `score.py` | the tools. Change a number in `grades.json`, re-run, and see the result |

**Law of this document.** The *display targets* (hex values the screen must show, grey-card readings, APL) are the law.
Parameter values are the best starting point to hit them. If the real assets make a number miss, move the parameter
until the target is hit. Never move the target.

## 0. The idea of the lens, in one paragraph

You are a passenger, not a camera operator. The lens therefore never moves on its own: no shake, no roll, no pitch,
no depth of field, and no lens artefacts in VR. Everything cinematic comes from the three things a real
cinematographer controls on a night bus: **what the window frames, where the light comes from, and how the image is
printed**. The story is a descent through the indigo vat. We start in the darkest dip (褐, the near-black night of a
rainy rural stop), rise through moonlight, pass through the one frame that is not blue (the violet tear) and the one
frame that is black (the plunge), and live one whole day in the dream: dawn, noon under the sea, dusk, and the stars.
The day ends in the palest dip of all, **藍白**, the colour the world is named after, filling the whole field of view.
Then the lie: the ordinary night again, drained of colour like an old mono recording. Then the truth: the same stop,
and one faint band of 藍白 on the real horizon.

## 1. The grammar: rules every scene obeys

1. **One white per frame, and it is where the eye must go.** Each scene has exactly one brightest element, and it is
   placed in the windscreen cone (azimuth -35°..+35° from the bus heading) unless noted. In order: the stop lamp, the
   headlight tunnel, the moon, the tear, (nothing), the sun, Snell's window, the sun in the slot between two mirrors,
   the rising mirror line, the white itself, the inverted lamps, the stop lamp.
2. **Blue lives in every shadow, including the warm scenes.** Warm hues are never allowed in shadows. At dusk the
   shadows are blue-violet and the warmth is only in light.
3. **Warm is rationed. Warm means earth, human, the world you leave.** It is allowed only in real-world practicals
   (cabin tubes, sodium streetlights, headlights, tail lamps, radio dial), the two suns (dawn, dusk), the palace
   vermilion in the deep, and the fireflies. Far warm lights are always small.
4. **Violet appears once**, as the colour of the Other (the tear, scene 4), with a faint echo as 紫贝 in the deep and in
   dusk cloud shadows. No other scene may carry a violet cast.
5. **No large area is pure black or pure white.** Any area larger than 2% of the frame stays between `#04060B` and
   `#F4F7FA`. Small emitters (lamp heads, sun, moon, tear core, star points, mirror lines) may clip. The grades build
   in a blue-black floor: input 0 displays as about `#02060C`, and the darkest real content (a night sky at linear
   0.003) lands on L0 `#070B14`. The ink never reaches zero, and red is never crushed. This also limits OLED black
   smear in VR.
6. **Horizon level, always.** The bus never rolls or pitches. Falling is shown by things rising past the windows. Any
   speed change takes at least 3 s with ease-in/out.
7. **The grade follows the sound's recording history.** Klemperer 1951 (mono, radio) is graded *archival*:
   desaturated, softer, grain on desktop. From the lift-off the sound "opens", and the grade opens with it. The Walter
   1958 stereo finale is full colour. The false awakening returns to the archival grade exactly, because "normal"
   is the old recording.
8. **VR is the eye, desktop is the camera.** Vignette and grain exist only in a desktop-only lens layer. There is no
   chromatic aberration, depth of field, motion blur, lens distortion, anamorphic bloom or letterbox anywhere.
9. **Photosensitivity.** No full-field luminance change greater than 1.5 EV happens faster than 0.25 s, except the one
   authored tear flash (section 5.5). No flicker faster than 3 cycles per second (tube flicker, mirror pulses).
   Measure in desktop mode.

## 3. Global technical setup (read before touching any scene)

### 3.1 Tonemapper: **Neutral**, in every profile. Colour Grading mode: **High Definition Range**.

Why not ACES:
- (a) Every pre-rendered asset we deliver (cloud cards, horizon strips) is display-referred through a neutral curve
  (Khronos PBR Neutral). ACES would re-compress and re-saturate them. The README already warned about this.
- (b) ACES skews saturated blues toward purple/cyan and darkens mid-grey by about 1 EV. Blue is our spine, and we
  need it exact.
- (c) PPv2 cannot interpolate the tonemapper enum between volumes. One tonemapper for the whole world means every
  crossfade is smooth.

PPv2 Neutral is nearly identity up to linear 0.3 (0.18 → display 118/255) and rolls off to clip at about linear 4.0.
The "film" contrast is added deliberately with the log-space Contrast slider. Do not use ACES anywhere, even for one
scene.

### 3.2 PostProcessLayer (on the VRC reference camera)

Volume layer = a dedicated `PostProcessing` layer. Volume trigger = the camera. Anti-aliasing = **None** (MSAA from
VRChat quality settings; TAA ghosts in VR). Stop NaN Propagation = on. Directly to Camera Target = off. Dithering stays
on (it breaks banding in the dark gradients: important for the night skies and the fog).

Reference camera: near clip 0.05 m, far clip 12 000 m (the colossi and the 4 km cloud ring must not be clipped).
Clear flags = Skybox.

### 3.3 Volume architecture and how to crossfade

- One **global** PostProcessVolume per profile in `grades.json`. `priority` = timeline order (S1 = 1 … S12 = 15).
  Helper volumes (FLASH_TEAR, WHITE_RAMP, MIRROR_PULSE) have priority 90-100. Desktop lens volumes have 200-201.
- **Crossfade rule** (PPv2 blends in priority order starting from defaults): the outgoing volume stays at weight 1.
  The incoming, higher-priority volume goes weight 0→1. When it reaches 1, set the outgoing volume to 0. Never fade
  one down while the other fades up. That would blend through PPv2 defaults and flash an ungraded frame.
- **Every profile overrides the same full parameter set**: Color Grading (mode, tonemapper, temperature, tint,
  postExposure, colorFilter, hueShift, saturation, contrast, lift, gamma, gain) and Bloom (intensity, threshold,
  softKnee, clamp, diffusion, anamorphicRatio, color, fastMode). Vignette and Grain are overridden with intensity 0 in
  scene profiles. If one profile forgets an override, fades pass through defaults.
- Drive weights from the timeline Udon behaviour (`PostProcessVolume.weight`; if that property is not exposed in your
  SDK build, animate the weights with an Animator on the volume objects). Use the curve named in section 6.
- **Desktop lens layer.** Two volumes, `LENS_ARCHIVE` and `LENS_CLEAN` (vignette + grain only; values in
  `grades.json`). On start: `if (Networking.LocalPlayer.IsUserInVR())` set both GameObjects inactive. In desktop,
  ARCHIVE runs in scenes 1, 2, 3 until the cloud entry, and 11. CLEAN runs everywhere else. Crossfade ARCHIVE→CLEAN
  inside the cloud whiteout (3) and CLEAN→ARCHIVE under the 藍白 overlay (10→11).
- Trackballs are PPv2 `Vector4` values (x, y, z = wheel colour with max channel 1, w = offset). Set them by script from
  `grades.json`. The inspector wheel is too coarse for these small offsets. Night-scene lift is
  `(0.9875, 0.9905, 1, 0.008)`: it lifts true black to about `#02060C`, a blue-black, without ever crushing red to 0.
  **Do not** tint lift toward blue with a plain wheel drag: the wheel subtracts luminance, so red goes negative, and
  the night blacks turn digital teal (`#00070B`). I measured this and rejected it.

### 3.4 Pre-rendered assets entering the grade (cloud cards, horizon strips)

The cards are display-referred. Pushed through PPv2 raw, their highlights are compressed a second time (a card pixel
at sRGB 0.93 comes back at 0.785), and dawn clouds go grey. That is exactly the "muddy dawn" complaint again. Give the card shader
an **inverse-Neutral** step, so that at a neutral grade the card displays exactly as authored and the scene grade then
acts on it like on everything else:

```hlsl
// PPv2 Neutral tonemapper, inverted per channel (closed form). In: display-linear (texture after sRGB decode).
float3 InverseNeutralPPv2(float3 y)
{
    const float a=0.2, b=0.29, c=0.24, d=0.272, e=0.02, f=0.3, whiteLevel=5.3;
    // whiteScale = 1 / NeutralCurve(5.3)
    float ws = 1.0 / (((whiteLevel*(a*whiteLevel+c*b)+d*e)/(whiteLevel*(a*whiteLevel+b)+d*f)) - e/f);
    y = min(y, 0.97);
    float3 v = y / ws + e / f;                  // undo post-scale and offset
    float3 A = a*v - a, B = b*v - c*b, C = d*f*v - d*e;
    float3 u = (-B - sqrt(max(B*B - 4*A*C, 0))) / (2*A);   // A<0 on [0,1): this is the positive root
    return max(u / ws, 0);
}
// card colour = InverseNeutralPPv2(tex.rgb * vertexColor.rgb) * _Gain;   _Gain = 1 by default
```

(Verified against the emulator: `y = 0.05 → 0.047`, `0.18 → 0.178`, `0.5 → 0.661`, `0.8 → 1.739`, `0.95 → 3.150`.
The other root of the quadratic is always negative. If you see negatives, you took the wrong sign.) If the materials lead prefers not to write a shader, the fallback is
`Particles/Standard Unlit` as delivered, with the scene's postExposure raised +0.3 EV. The clouds then lose a little
top-end sparkle. The mock-ups in section 10 use the inverse-Neutral path.

### 3.5 Light units in Built-in: the three traps

1. **Intensity is applied in gamma space** (Built-in default, `GraphicsSettings.lightsUseLinearIntensity = false`;
   do not change it, because the VRChat client uses its own). Linear irradiance E = colour_lin × intensity^2.2.
   Inspector intensity 2 is 4.6× the light of 1, not 2×. Every table below gives **E (linear)** and the **inspector
   value = E^(1/2.2)** for directional lights.
2. **Baked (Progressive) point/spot lights use inverse-square falloff; realtime ones use Unity's legacy range
   falloff.** Do not copy intensities between a baked light and its realtime twin. Calibrate each with the grey card.
3. **The grey card.** Make a hidden prefab: a 0.5 m quad, Standard shader, albedo `#767676` (= 0.18 linear),
   smoothness 0. Plus a **white card** (albedo `#E6E6E6` ≈ 0.80, smoothness 0). Section 11.2 lists what each card must
   read in a screenshot under each light. This is the only reliable way to set light levels in a world whose skies are
   unlit textures.

### 3.6 Lighting architecture for a camera rig that travels through twelve worlds

- **The bus interior is realtime/probe-lit, never lightmapped.** Its light changes in every scene. Bake the bus AO in
  Blender into the occlusion maps (grounding and contact shadows), and light it with:
  - (a) `RenderSettings` ambient in **Trilight** mode, set per scene by Udon (values in each scene below);
  - (b) one realtime **directional "world key"** (moon or sun), soft shadows, shadow distance 25 m, culling mask =
    Bus + Tentacles + Player, so the window frames throw moving shadow stripes;
  - (c) cabin tube lights: 2 realtime point lights, no shadows, used in 1-4 and 11-12;
  - (d) one realtime **sweep spot** for passing streetlights (2 and 11).
  At most 1 shadowed + 3 unshadowed pixel lights touch the bus at any time.
- **The ground world (road, stop, fields, 1/2/11/12) is baked** (the stop lamp, the streetlights, the sky), with light
  probes around the bus so the probe-lit bus sits in it.
- **The sky worlds are unlit** (skybox, cards, strips, sea). The only lit objects in them are the tentacles and the bus.
- **Reflections**: set `RenderSettings.customReflection` (or one baked probe that follows the bus, switched per scene)
  to that scene's sky cubemap, so chrome, glass and the wet road reflect the right world. The glass must show the
  cabin reflected at 4-8% at night (scenes 2 and 11). That double exposure is half the "night bus" look. Bake a cabin
  cubemap with the tubes on, and blend it into the window glass while the tubes are on.

### 3.7 Fog: one mode, everywhere: **Exponential**

Exponential is physically right for rain, aerial perspective and water (Beer-Lambert). Using a single mode also
avoids stripped fog variants (Graphics Settings → Shader Stripping → Fog Modes = Custom, tick Exponential only).
Density d gives 50% fog at 0.693/d metres and 90% at 2.303/d metres.

Rules:
- **Fog colour = the skybox colour at the horizon in that direction.** If they differ, far objects float as cut-outs.
  Sample the skybox at 0-2° elevation in the main view direction and use the "set" value from section 11.3.
- **Sky objects never take fog**: moon, sun, tear, stars, cloud ring strips, town lights. Put them on fog-free shaders
  with their own distance fade, or they vanish at km distances.
- Fog colour and density are animated by Udon (`RenderSettings.fogColor`, `fogDensity`).

### 3.8 Bloom and the emission budget

PPv2 bloom: threshold is in **gamma** space (threshold 1.0 = linear 1.0). Intensity maps to a gain of 2^(I/10) − 1
(I = 4 → 0.32, 6 → 0.52, 10 → 1.0, 20 → 3.0), summed over about 7 pyramid levels. So a large bright area blooms far
more than a point. Budget:

- **Non-emissive surfaces stay below linear 0.9 after lighting**, so they never bloom. Specular glints on wet asphalt,
  chrome and tentacle rims may exceed it: that is the halation we want.
- Emitters that should glow sit at 4-16× (+2..+4 EV) over the threshold. Values are given per scene.
- **Text never blooms**: poem text material ≤ linear 0.85. Blurry text in VR is unreadable.
- Diffusion 6-9 (8 in rain/haze for wide soft halos; 6 for stars, which must stay pin-sharp).

### 3.9 Calibration kit (what the implementer does once per scene)

1. Build the **grade test card**: 11 unlit quads, fog off, emission = grey 0.18 × 2^k for k = -6..+4. Put it on a
   hidden debug layer in front of the camera. Screenshot it with each profile at weight 1. It must match the ramp in
   section 11.4 within ±3 codes. This proves the profile is set right, independent of the art.
2. Place the grey card and white card under each named light (section 11.2) and screenshot. Adjust light intensity
   until the readings match.
3. Set fog, sky and emitter colours from section 11.3.
4. Measure the **APL**: the mean Rec.709 luma of the desktop frame (1920×1080, hFOV 90°, seated head position, looking
   straight forward). Compare it with the value score (section 4). Tolerance ±15%. Fix it with postExposure only if
   steps 1-3 are done.

Four shapes to see in this score:

- **Brightness is not loudness.** The brightest dream scene (dawn) is quiet horns. The loudest (the fff climax) is

- **The day inside the dream.** Temperature swings warm (dawn) → cool (noon under water) → **warm (dusk: the only warm

- **Saturation tells the recording's story.** -15 (the stop) → **-22** (mono radio) → -6 (the sound opens) → +4 (the
  stereo finale: colour is born) → **+8** (dusk: the most colour) → 0 (stars) → 0 (white) → **-22** (false wake:
  archival again) → -8 (waking: the dream leaves a trace of colour in the real world).
- **Contrast peaks twice**: at the gate (+22, terror) and in the deep (+16, faith in darkness). It falls to **0 at the
  white**, where the grade itself dissolves.

## 5. Scene by scene

Conventions: azimuth 0° = bus heading, + = right; elevation above the horizon. "Seat" = the player's seat, assumed
**row 2, right side (door side), window seat**, eye 1.15 m above the floor (≈ 2.1 m above the road). From there the
windscreen spans about azimuth -45°..+20° with its centre near -12°. The empty driver's seat and wheel sit low front-left.
The door glass is front-right. The right side window is 0.4 m from the face. If the seat is already fixed elsewhere,
keep the azimuths relative to the heading and re-check the framing notes.

Light tables give colour, E (linear irradiance on a card facing the light), the inspector value for a directional
light (gamma rule), and what the grey card must read after the grade. Palette hexes are **display targets**. Where an
element is unlit or emissive, section 11.3 gives the value to type in.

### 5.1 站牌 The stop (spawn → boarding)

**Key image.** A single cold-white lamp on a leaning pole over a rusted blue plate reading 「蓝白」, its light drawn as a
long white stroke on the black mirror of the wet road; beside it the old white-and-blue minibus glows cream from
inside, doors open, headlight beams dissolving into blue fog, drizzle visible only where it crosses the light.

**Camera / viewing.**
- Spawn standing on the road shoulder **13 m back from the stop**, eye 1.6 m, facing the stop at **28° to the road
  axis**, so the first frame is composed:
  - the lamp head upper-left third (x 0.35, y 0.22 of a 16:9 frame);
  - the sign below it (x 0.35, y 0.45);
  - the bus body running x 0.45 → 0.95 with the open door at x 0.55 (a warm vertical rectangle);
  - the headlight cones leaving the right edge into fog;

  - the field line / horizon at y 0.52.
- Eye path: **lamp → sign → door → beams (where you will go)**. Keep everything behind the spawn dark and empty
  (fog, field), so nothing competes.
- The player walks about 12 m through the light to the door. Once seated, the lamp and sign are framed in the door
